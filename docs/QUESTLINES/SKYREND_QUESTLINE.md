# SKYREND — THE UNREAD SKY (Skyrend chapter questline)

**Written to the Quest Doctrine v1.0. Theology in play (shown, never told):** the storm-reading — truth is told to your face, and oaths are sworn in the open rain.
**Chapter wound:** the storm-vanes have stopped turning. Haeldor's Circuit is broken — the sky is lying now, and every island reads the sky by what its roofline does.

### Q1 — "THE VANES"
- **HOOK:** A vane-warden's father's funeral is interrupted by stillness — no wind in the city, the great vane frozen mid-swing. He asks the pilgrim to climb and free it. *(Want: the funeral done. Lie: he hasn't climbed since his brother fell from the vane. Cost: his fear, in front of the whole grieving town.)*
- **TRAIL:** The climb past clogged hinge-stones — Grimlight motes nest where the vanes should turn (anti-witnesses stopping the witnesses). The streets below read the frozen vane as an omen; some are already leaving.
- **RITE:** Freeing the vane and reading the FIRST TRUE WEATHER of the chapter aloud — the town reads the sky by what the roofline does (saga-lesson performed).
- **CHOICE (two goods):** Point the freed vane true (the town learns the storm is coming — panic, evacuation, lives saved, crops abandoned) or let it swing wide (calm streets, but the storm arrives unannounced). **The funeral finishes under either sky.**
- **ECHO:** The vane turns or it doesn't, forever on the skyline; the warden climbs again or doesn't.
- **TRUTH-SLIVER:** The vanes imitate the giant's passing. (Haeldor's Circuit, learned by fixing one hinge of it.)

### Q2 — "THE OPEN RAIN"
- **HOOK:** Two Skyrend families dispute a sky-border at the rain-oath stone; oaths sworn in the open rain — but the rain refuses to fall on one family's word. The matriarch asks the pilgrim to stand witness. *(Want: the border. Lie: she cut a road through a shrine grove to move it. Cost: her family's name, soaked in front of everyone.)*
- **TRAIL:** The shrine grove's stumps still hold rain differently — the weather remembers where the shrine stood.
- **RITE:** The rain-oath — both families speak in the open rain and the sky answers one of them.
- **CHOICE (two goods):** Expose the old crime (truth stands, the family loses everything — storm-reading demands truth) or keep the secret (the border-oath fails, the families feud a generation).
- **ECHO:** The oath-stone's rain-shadow marks the ruling; both houses treat the pilgrim by it.
- **TRUTH-SLIVER:** Oaths are read by the SKY, not by men. (The storm-reading, learned by standing in it.)

### Q3 — "THE WIND-SINGERS' BREATH"
- **HOOK:** The choir that names storms has gone mute mid-name; a half-named storm stalks the ridges. A young singer asks the pilgrim to help finish the name — only the storm knows the rest. *(Want: her master's last name finished. Lie: the master died mid-name and she blames herself for the breath. Cost: finishing it means singing the storm's grief instead of her own.)*
- **TRAIL:** The half-named storm circles the Circuit's vanes; its unnamed edge hollows what it touches (Hollow-weather, witnessed).
- **RITE:** Singing the storm's name on the Circuit — the pilgrim carries the refrain line (rite participation, no music skill needed: the town sings through you).
- **CHOICE (two goods):** Name the storm after the dead master (honor; the storm calms over his shrine; the singer stays bound to the choir) or let the singer take the name herself (she's freed, the storm follows HER road — the sky now belongs to someone younger).
- **ECHO:** The storm is steered or it wanders; the singer greets the pilgrim as heir or as free wind.
- **TRUTH-SLIVER:** Naming a storm steers it. The singers' power is witness, not magic. (Learned by holding a refrain.)

### Q4 — "THE CALM EYE"
- **HOOK:** Pilgrimage to the still center of the world-hurricane: Sylwenna's weather-glass has turned clear — the day it turns clear, the world should apologize for something. A weather-reader asks the pilgrim to look first. *(Want: to not be the one who sees. Lie: he already knows what it shows. Cost: whoever carries the warning owns its consequences.)*
- **TRAIL:** The Eye's mirror-surface reflects not the sky but the land below the clouds — a wound in the March's belief, a Hollow nest in the wind's roots.
- **RITE:** Reading the glass — the pilgrim holds the frame while it shows the wound (the queen's vigil, performed).
- **CHOICE (two goods):** Carry the warning to the gate-capital first (panic, evacuation, safe people, a broken season) or to the singers first (the warning becomes a named storm steered away — but a fishing fleet rides the diverted edge).
- **ECHO:** The Eye reflects the choice; the evacuated streets or the lost fleet appear in the glass on return.
- **TRUTH-SLIVER:** The calm eye sees everything; the queen's silence is curation, not absence. (Sylwenna, learned by looking where she looks.)

### Q5 — "THE SQUALL GATE" (chapter finale)
- **THE ROLL CALL at the Gate:** the vane-warden, the ruling family's heir, the singer (bound or free), the weather-reader, the fleets or the evacuated.
- **THE RITE:** The pilgrim speaks the March's named storms back to the Gate — a season of weather recited as worship.
- **THE INTERRUPTION:** The storm breaks wrong — an **ECHO shaped like a storm that was never named** rises (T3). Three phases; **final phase drops to pilgrim scale**: the pilgrim climbs into the false storm's eye to break the unnamed heart.
- **THE WAKING:** HAELDOR WAKES THROUGH THE PILGRIM — the storm parts, the Circuit turns again, the March's sky is readable once more.
- **TRUTH-SLIVER:** The sky was never weather. It was a god's attention. (Learned by being under both.)

## SIDE HOOKS
- **"The Downed Vane"** — one out-island's vane fell in the break; carrying it back up the cliff is a Wayfarer road-quest (class flavor, FAITH stream).
- **"The Rain-Scribe"** — a Cantor who records oaths by rain-shadow asks the pilgrim to witness a dying man's last promise (two-goods in miniature).

## DEPTHS TIE-IN
**The Skyrend Depths** = the Storm-King's dreamed thunderheads — layouts of cloud-chambers solid enough to walk, lightning frozen mid-arc. Depth fragments: a weather-glass shard that shows tomorrow's grief (seen, not read).
